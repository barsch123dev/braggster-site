# Play Board Games Together on Several Phones, Wi-Fi or Online

A board game on one phone works fine until the phone has to cross the table every turn. Braggster's
Play together mode gives every player their own phone and keeps one live match on all of them. Play
on the Wi-Fi you are already on, with no internet needed, or online with friends anywhere. This guide
covers how to set it up, which games support it, and what to check when a table will not show up.

## Three ways to play with friends

**One phone, passed around.** Every playable board in Braggster works as pass and play on a single
device. It needs nothing but the phone, and it is still the quickest way to start a game of Chess or
Dominoes.

**Play together, a phone each.** One phone hosts a table and the others join it. Everyone sees the
same board and the same scores, updated live, and plays their own seat from their own phone.

**Same Wi-Fi or online.** Players in the same room join over the same Wi-Fi, and players somewhere
else join online. One table can hold both kinds at once.

## Same Wi-Fi or online: what is the difference

- **On the same Wi-Fi**, you do not need the internet at all. The phones talk to each other directly
  over your local network, the connection is encrypted, and a table that stays on Wi-Fi never touches
  a server.
- **Online** you need an internet connection. Friends join with an invite link or an 8-character code,
  and the moves pass through Braggster's relay server in the EU. The match is end-to-end encrypted:
  the relay only passes it on and cannot read it, and players never see each other's internet
  address. See the [privacy page](/privacy/) for the rest.
- **Mixed.** Two players on the sofa can scan the code while a third joins from another city, all at
  the same table.

Either way there is no account to create, no username and no friend list.

## Step by step: start a table and join it

1. **The host opens a game** that supports Play together and taps **Play together**, then
   **Start a table**. The host's phone shows a QR code and an 8-character code, and becomes the table.
2. **Players in the same room scan it.** On the same Wi-Fi, they open Braggster, tap
   **Join or start** and then **Join**, choose **Scan the code** and point the camera at the host's
   screen.
3. **Or pick it from the list.** The table also appears under **Found on this Wi-Fi**, so a player
   can tap it there instead of scanning.
4. **Players elsewhere get an invite.** The host taps **Send an invite** and sends the link through
   WhatsApp or any other app. The friend taps the link. Or the host reads out the code, and the friend
   taps **Join** and then **Type the code**. If the host switches apps to send the invite, the table
   waits, and everyone rejoins by themselves.
5. **The host lets people in.** A scanned QR code lets a player straight in. Anyone who picked the
   table from the list, or joined by link or code, waits until the host taps **Accept**, unless the
   host switched on **Join without approval**.
6. **Keep the table yours.** The host can use **Lock table** once everyone is in, make a **New link**
   (old links and codes stop working), or remove a player.
7. **Start the match.** Once everyone is seated, the host starts, and each player plays their own
   seat on their own phone. When the match ends the table stays open: tap **Play again**, and
   everyone who says yes plays the next match in the same seats.

## Which games you can play together

- **[Yahtzee](/games/yahtzee/)**, for up to six players. The host can switch on digital dice for the
  table: whoever's turn it is throws on their own phone, and everyone else watches the dice land on
  theirs. Each player fills in their own column. See the
  [Yahtzee scoring guide](/blog/how-to-score-yahtzee/).
- **[Chess](/games/chess/)**, with every legal move enforced on both boards.
- **[Checkers](/games/checkers/)** and **[International draughts](/games/dammen/)**, including long
  capture sequences.
- **[Backgammon](/games/backgammon/)**, with singles, gammons and backgammons scored as usual. See the
  [Backgammon scoring guide](/blog/how-to-score-backgammon/).
- **[Othello](/games/reversi/)**, where every flip lands on both screens.

## iPhone and Android at one table

A table can mix iPhones and Android phones freely, phones and tablets alike. Play together uses Wi-Fi
or the internet rather than Bluetooth, and that is exactly what lets the two kinds of phone meet.

Joining is free, online as well. Only the host needs the game: a player who joins does not have to
own it or have any free plays left, and joining never uses one up, so one person's phone never holds
up the table.

## Does it count in your statistics?

Yes. A Play together match is saved on every phone at the table and counts in each player's own
statistics, win rate and streaks, just like a match played on one phone. Practice games against the
computer are the exception: those never count, on any device.

## When the table does not show up

Nearly every problem comes down to the phones not being able to see each other on the network, and
there is a way around it.

- **Allow the local network on iPhone.** The first time you start or join a table, iOS asks whether
  Braggster may use the local network, "to host and join a shared scorecard with other players on the
  same WiFi". Tap Allow. If you declined, turn Local Network back on for Braggster in the iPhone's
  Settings.
- **Scan the code instead of using the list.** The list of tables relies on the phones finding each
  other automatically, and some networks quietly block that. Scanning the QR code connects directly.
- **Use your home Wi-Fi.** Guest networks, and many hotel, office and school networks, keep devices
  apart from each other on purpose. When that happens nothing can connect on the Wi-Fi, not even
  with a scanned code. Switch to an ordinary home Wi-Fi network, or join online instead.
- **Join online by invite link or code.** If the Wi-Fi keeps the phones apart, or one friend is on
  mobile data, the host taps **Send an invite** and the others open the link, or tap **Join** and
  **Type the code**. Everyone needs an internet connection for this. If Braggster says it could not
  reach the online table service, check the internet connection on that phone.
- **Check everyone is on the same network.** A phone that has dropped back to mobile data, or joined
  a different Wi-Fi in the same house, will not find the table in the list. It can still join with the
  link or the code.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can two people play chess on two phones?**
Yes. With Play together, one phone starts a table and the other joins over the same Wi-Fi, or online
with an invite link. Both players see the board live and each moves from their own phone.

**Can we play online with friends who are not in the same room?**
Yes. Start a table, tap **Send an invite** and send the link in WhatsApp or any other app, or read out
the 8-character code. It works with Yahtzee, Chess, Checkers, International draughts, Backgammon and
Othello. There is no account to create, joining is free, and the match is end-to-end encrypted, so the
server only passes it on and cannot read it.

**Do we need an internet connection?**
Not on the same Wi-Fi: the phones only need to be on the same network, and nothing goes over the
internet. To play online with friends who are somewhere else, everyone needs an internet connection.
Either way there is no account to create.

**Can iPhone and Android play together?**
Yes. A table can mix iPhones and Android phones freely, and players on the same Wi-Fi and players
online can share one table.

**How many players can join a table?**
Yahtzee takes up to six players, a phone each. Chess, Checkers, International draughts, Backgammon
and Othello are two-player games, so two phones.

**Does everyone need to buy the game?**
No. Only the host needs the game. Joining a table is free, on Wi-Fi and online.

**Why can't my friend see the table?**
Check that both phones are on the same Wi-Fi and, on an iPhone, that Braggster may use the local
network. If the table still does not appear, scan the host's code. On a guest or hotel network the
phones may be kept apart entirely, so switch to a home Wi-Fi, or send an invite link or the code and
join online instead.
