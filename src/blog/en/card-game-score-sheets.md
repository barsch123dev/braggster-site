# Card Game Score Sheets: How to Keep Score for 33 Card Games

Almost every card game worth playing twice needs a score sheet, and almost every household keeps
score the same way: a torn-off page, a pen that has gone missing, and an argument about who wrote
down the 40 in the wrong column. Braggster replaces that page with a calculating scorecard built for
the specific game you are playing, so the total is right by construction rather than by arithmetic.

This guide covers how scoring actually works across the main card game families, and which of the 33
card games in Braggster fit each one.

## What a card game score sheet has to do

A generic table of numbers is the wrong tool for most card games. A real score sheet has to know:

- **Who the sides are.** Klaverjassen and Bridge are fixed partnerships, so the sheet has two
  columns, not four. Poker and Pesten are individuals, so it has one per player.
- **What a round is worth.** A Klaverjassen round distributes exactly 162 card points plus roem.
  A Whist round scores only the tricks above the book of six. A Canasta round can swing thousands.
- **Which direction wins.** Most games are highest total wins. Burro, Golf-style games and every
  puzzle in the app are lowest wins, and a score sheet that gets this backwards ranks the loser first.
- **When the game ends.** Race to 500, race to 11, sixteen rounds, or first to run out of cards.

Braggster's scorecards encode all four per game, which is why there are 33 of them rather than one
spreadsheet.

## Trick taking games

The largest family by far. Players follow suit, the highest card takes the trick, and the score
comes from the cards captured or the tricks won.

**Point capture.** The deck carries a fixed number of card points that get divided each hand.
Klaverjassen splits 162 points plus roem between two teams. [Jass](/games/) does the same across 36
cards with Weis melds and a trump multiplier. Belote, Briscola, Scopa, Skat, Doppelkopf, Schnapsen,
Königrufen and Cuarenta all work on this principle with different decks and different targets.

**Trick counting.** The cards have no individual value: you score for the number of tricks.
Whist scores every trick above the book of six. Twenty-Five gives five points a trick and races to
25. All Fours scores High, Low, Jack and Game to 14.

**Bid and make.** You predict your tricks first and get punished for missing.
Bridge, Spades, 500, Bollen and Euchre all live here. Spades adds Nil bids and a bag penalty every
ten overtricks, which is exactly the sort of rule a paper sheet forgets and a calculating scorecard
does not.

**Avoidance.** Hearts inverts the whole idea: you are trying not to capture, and shooting the moon
flips the round on its head.

## Rummy and melding games

Draw, discard, build sets and runs, and score the cards you did or did not get rid of.

Rummy, Jokeren and Canasta are the three in Braggster. Canasta is the extreme case: two decks plus
jokers, a target of 5000, and round values large enough that mental arithmetic stops being reliable
around the third hand. The Buraco variant shares the same engine, so the sheet is the same whichever
name your family uses.

## Shedding and matching games

Get rid of your cards first. The scoring is usually a penalty count of what everyone else still holds.

Pesten is the Dutch version of Crazy Eights and the one most Dutch households actually play.
Uno and Uno Flip are the commercial cousins, each with its own rules screen spelling out what every
action card does. Burro is the elimination version: every lost round adds a letter, five letters
puts you out, and fewest letters leads, so this one ranks lowest wins.

Deep dive: [Pesten scoring and rules](/blog/how-to-score-pesten/).

## Hand comparison games

Poker and Blackjack are scored by outcome, never by anything staked. Braggster's Poker sheet scores
finishing place per sit down: the winner takes one point per player at the table and last place takes
one. Blackjack scores the result of the hand: a blackjack is 3, a win is 2, a push is 1, a loss is 0.

That design is deliberate. Braggster records results, never amounts. There is nothing anywhere in the
app that stands in for money.

Blackjack also plays for real inside the app: it deals hands against the dealer, hit and stand, and
scores the result through the same round path as a hand entered by keyboard.

## Solo card games

Solitaire, or Patience, is one player against the deal. Braggster scores it Vegas or standard style
by default, and its in app mode opens a full tap to move Klondike board that scores the finished deal
straight into your record.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need an account to keep score?**
No. Braggster is local first: matches, players and statistics live on your device, there is no login,
and there is no backend to sign up for.

**Can I use it offline?**
Yes. Nothing in the scoring path needs a connection.

**What if my family plays a house variant?**
Most games ship variant rules relative to the base game (Amsterdam and Rotterdam Klaverjassen,
Klondike draw one and draw three, Block, Draw and All Fives Dominoes, and so on). If yours is not
covered, the always free Blank scorecard lets you keep score for anything.

**Does it track statistics over time?**
Yes. Every finished match feeds a per player record, so you can settle the question of who actually
wins most often.

**Are there ads?**
No ads, and no external payment processors. Purchases go through the App Store and Google Play only.
